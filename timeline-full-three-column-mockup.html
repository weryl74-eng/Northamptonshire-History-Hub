Era I

Prehistory

Before AD 43
Northamptonshire in this period

Long before Northampton or its castles existed, the land that became Northamptonshire was already shaped by human hands — hillforts, burial mounds and the earliest farming settlements across the county's ironstone ridges and river valleys.

Era II

Roman

AD 43–410
Northamptonshire in this period

Roman roads cut straight through the county, linking market towns and villas that grew wealthy on the surrounding farmland. Traces of this era still surface in Northamptonshire fields and gardens today.

Era III

Anglo-Saxon

410–1066
Northamptonshire in this period

Northampton emerged as a fortified Saxon burh and a strategic seat of Mercian power, while villages across the shire took root under the administrative pattern that still shapes the county map.

Era IV

Medieval

1066–1485
Northamptonshire in this period

Castles, abbeys and a thriving wool trade defined medieval Northamptonshire. Northampton Castle hosted kings and church trials, while Delapré Abbey and the county's market towns grew in influence.

Era V

Tudor & Stuart

1485–1714
Northamptonshire in this period

Civil War fighting reached the county at Naseby, Puritan communities took hold, and the seeds of Northampton's boot and shoe trade were quietly sown in this turbulent, transformative period.

Era VI

Georgian & Victorian

1714–1901
Northamptonshire in this period

Canals and railways arrived, market towns expanded, and Northamptonshire became the beating heart of Britain's footwear industry — a legacy still written into the county's streets, factories and museums.

20th Century to Today

1901–Present
Northamptonshire in this period
